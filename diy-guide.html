How to run your own holiday home in Abu Dhabi

The complete, honest guide from a team that runs holiday homes here every day. Every step from empty apartment to paying guests, how much of your time each one takes, and where most owners get it wrong.

Holiday homes can earn more than a yearly lease. They also take real work.

Abu Dhabi's holiday home market is growing fast, and the rules got stricter at the same time. No DCT licence means no listing, on any platform.

This guide shows you everything, including the parts most people skip. Read it, then decide how much of it you want to do yourself.

1

Make sure the unit works for guests

Start with two questions: is the unit allowed to operate as a holiday home, and will guests actually want to stay there?

  • You rent the whole unit, never single rooms. Maximum three guests per bedroom.
  • Owners, joint owners and companies can apply. Tenants can too, with the owner's authorisation.
  • Guests book for convenience: a pool and gym, parking, and being close to the airport, beaches, attractions and event venues.
Saadiyat IslandBeaches, museums and a quieter, upscale feel.
Yas IslandTheme parks, the F1 circuit, concerts, golf, and close to the airport.
Al Reem IslandCity centre, business trips, shopping and dining.
Al RahaWaterfront living, near Yas and the airport.
Masdar CityNear the airport, popular with business and longer stays.

Units that offer something special earn more: a golf course or sea view, a beachfront, a striking design, or an experience guests can't get in a hotel.

Where owners get it wrong: assuming any apartment will book well, without asking who will stay there and why.

2

Get your DCT licence and insurance

The Department of Culture and Tourism Abu Dhabi (DCT) licenses every holiday home. You apply online, pay the fees, and the unit is inspected before you can start.

  • Documents: title deed (or lease and owner authorisation), passport or Emirates ID, and an insurance certificate covering the unit.
  • Operating as a business may also require an economic licence.
  • The licence is renewed every year, and its number must appear on every listing.

Insurance usually takes the longest. You need to find an insurer that covers holiday home use, understand what DCT requires, and compare quotes.

Where owners get it wrong: assuming their normal home insurance is enough. It usually isn't.

Your time
About 1 week

Once, then yearly
3

Prepare the unit for inspection

DCT checks safety, quality and amenities before approving the unit.

  • Smoke detectors, a fire extinguisher, a fire blanket and a first aid kit.
  • Clear emergency information for guests.
  • Everything working: AC, appliances, lights, locks.

Where owners get it wrong: failing the first inspection over one missing item, and losing more time waiting for the next visit.

Your time
About 2 days
How often
Once, then checks
4

Furnish for every kind of guest

Furnishing is your biggest competitive edge. Browse Airbnb or Booking.com in any area and you'll see it: better-furnished units charge more per night, fill more nights and collect better reviews.

Your guests come from many countries and cultures, so the design has to feel welcoming to all of them, while staying durable and easy to maintain.

It is also the most demanding step. A typical apartment needs around 280 items. That's 280 decisions, 280 payments and 280 deliveries, followed by assembly, fixing, styling and a deep clean, all coordinated across several teams.

Why we focus here: we studied what guests in the UAE respond to and worked with European designers on pieces that look good, last, and stay cost-effective.

Where owners get it wrong: furnishing it like their own home. It ends up looking like someone's apartment, not a place guests want to book.

5

Take photos that sell the stay

  • Use a professional photographer, in daylight, with every light on.
  • Photograph the pool, gym, view and anything special about the building or area.
  • Add close-up detail shots: bedding, the coffee corner, finishes.
  • Make beds and set out towels first, and remove anything personal.

Where owners get it wrong: dark phone photos that make a great unit look average.

Your time
Half a day
How often
Once
6

Create your listings

  • Lead with the bedroom. The first thing guests wonder is where they'll sleep.
  • Write a clear title, an honest description, house rules, and your DCT licence number.
  • Abu Dhabi charges a 6% tourism fee on short stays, which you collect and pay to DCT monthly.
  • Airbnb and Booking.com are the main platforms. To list on more, use a channel manager so calendars stay in sync.

Where owners get it wrong: double bookings from managing several platforms by hand.

Your time
4–6 hours
How often
Once, then updates
7

Price for the calendar, not just the week

Weekday and weekend pricing is the easy part. The real money is in special events.

Keep an eye on concerts, sports, exhibitions and holidays, and adjust for high season and summer.

Where owners get it wrong: missing an event. A big concert is in town, you're charging 400 a night, and similar units are getting 1,200.

Your time
About 30 minutes a day
How often
Every day
8

Look after your guests

Self check-in makes life much easier. Use a smart lock or lock box, and send a detailed message before arrival covering:

  • How to find the building and the unit.
  • Where the keys are, and what happens if they're lost.
  • Wi-Fi details, parking, and where the garbage room is.

Most communities require you to email them for every booking so the guest gets access. Guests arrive tired and sometimes emotional, so patience matters. Ask for feedback and act on it straight away.

Where owners get it wrong: the 2am message they didn't see, and the guest stuck at the gate because the community was never told.

Your time
30–60 minutes a day, and on call
How often
Every day
9

Clean and reset after every stay

  • You'll need a cleaning team and a separate laundry service.
  • Keep spare sets of sheets, towels, pillowcases and duvet covers so turnovers never wait on laundry.
  • Use a checklist for every clean, and have someone check it.

Where owners get it wrong: two checkouts on the same day and no clean linen ready.

Your time
Coordinating every checkout

Every booking
10

Keep everything working

  • Service the AC regularly. In Abu Dhabi, it matters more than anything else.
  • Have a handyman who can come the same day.
  • Replace worn or broken items between guests.

Where owners get it wrong: the AC failing in August with guests inside.

11

Handle the money and paperwork

  • Pay the tourism fee to DCT every month.
  • Track platform payouts, cleaning, laundry, repairs and bills.
  • Renew your licence and insurance every year.

Where owners get it wrong: never checking whether the unit actually earns more than a yearly lease would.

Insider notes

Small things that make a big difference, learned from running units day to day.

Keep supplies simple

Don't over-provide water, coffee and snacks. Some guests will take advantage, and it adds up quickly.

Refill, don't replace

Use refillable shampoo, conditioner and hand soap. If you store spares in the unit, keep them locked away.

Control the keys

Spare keys should only be held by your cleaning team, never left in the unit.

Have a lost and found policy

Guests will leave things behind. Decide in advance how long you keep items and how they get returned.

Watch the event calendar

One missed concert or race weekend can cost you more than a week of normal bookings.

Don't forget the community

A missed access email means a guest stuck at the gate. Automate it if you can.

Always have backup linen

Same-day turnovers only work if clean sets are ready before the dirty ones leave.

Stay patient

Most difficult guests are just tired travellers. A calm, quick reply saves most reviews.

Should you do it yourself?

Answer five quick questions honestly.

Can you reply to guests within minutes, any time of day?
Do you live close enough to reach the unit the same day?
Do you have a reliable cleaning team and laundry service?
Would you enjoy tracking events and adjusting prices every day?
Do you have 35+ spare hours every month?
